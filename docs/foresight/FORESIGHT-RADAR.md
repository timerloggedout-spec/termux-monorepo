# FOSS / Emerging Technology Foresight Radar

This radar turns the daily briefing into a standing horizon-scanning input for
'termux-monorepo'.

## Horizon lanes

- **H0 — Confirmed:** shipped releases, merged projects, published papers, verified incidents, stable standards, and directly observable ecosystem changes.
- **H1 — Emerging:** credible projects, research results, pilots, funding, standards work, or adoption signals that could become operationally relevant.
- **H2 — Weak signal:** early prototypes, unusual technical disclosures, niche FOSS projects, pre-standard work, or converging signals with limited validation.
- **H3 — Speculative:** concepts and scenarios worth tracking but not yet supported enough to treat as established developments.

## Radar dimensions

Every candidate should be tagged with one or more dimensions:

- enabling technology
- infrastructure
- compute / accelerator
- memory / storage
- energy / thermal
- agent / orchestration
- security / safety
- developer tooling
- FOSS ecosystem
- science / research
- materials / hardware
- procurement / sovereign capability
- interoperability / standards
- mobile / edge / Termux
- supply-chain / provenance

## Daily selection rule

Select five items that maximize information value rather than headline volume.
Prefer independent, primary evidence and diversity across radar dimensions.

Each item records:

1. headline
2. horizon lane
3. concise summary
4. evidence status
5. why it matters
6. practical opportunity / procurement implication
7. relevant Termux-monorepo connection
8. primary sources
9. relevant image
10. confidence and unresolved questions

Do not turn H1/H2/H3 signals into confirmed facts.

## Armasuisse / Envisioning-style treatment

Use the radar as a foresight instrument, not as a prediction engine. Look for:

- weak signals that recur across independent sources
- enabling technologies that unlock several downstream capabilities
- convergence between software, hardware, science, and policy
- dependencies that could become procurement bottlenecks
- technologies whose importance is disproportionate to current visibility
- FOSS projects that could reduce strategic/vendor lock-in
- concepts that deserve a watchlist before they deserve implementation

The radar must preserve uncertainty and evidence provenance.
