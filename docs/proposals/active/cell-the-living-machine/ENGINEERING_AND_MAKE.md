# Engineering, Automation and Real-World Making

## Product thesis
CELL: THE LIVING MACHINE grows into a creator platform where player ideas mature into engineering concepts, simulated systems, reviewed designs, prototypes and real-world production opportunities. The game provides an accessible entry point; the platform supplies deeper engineering tools and verified pathways into physical making.

## Engineering is a first-class system
The product combines biological engineering with general engineering and industrial systems:
- requirements, constraints, specifications and acceptance criteria;
- components, assemblies, materials, tolerances and interfaces;
- energy, mass, fluid, signal and information flows;
- production lines, automation, logistics, storage, throughput and bottlenecks;
- sensors, controls, feedback loops, robotics and machine operations;
- reliability, maintenance, safety, quality assurance and lifecycle cost;
- CAD, CAE, digital twins, simulation, manufacturing plans and revision control.

Factory-building and automation games such as Factorio provide a genre reference for resource networks, throughput, production chains, automation and optimization. The implementation creates original mechanics, art, names, progression and content.

## Play modes and player journeys
| Mode | Player activity | Engineering output |
|---|---|---|
| Exploration | Discover systems, inspect components, experiment freely and follow curiosity. | Observations, hypotheses, maps and saved discoveries. |
| Arcade / Challenge | Solve focused tasks with clear constraints, scoring and replay. | Tested solutions, performance traces and challenge records. |
| Engineering Studio | Design components, circuits, machines, organisms or production systems. | Versioned design files, bills of materials, schematics and simulations. |
| Factory / Automation | Connect production stages, logistics, control loops and quality checks. | Process graphs, capacity models and optimized production plans. |
| Research Lab | Investigate evidence, model mechanisms and compare hypotheses. | Research dossiers, source-linked claims and reproducible experiments. |
| Simulation / Digital Twin | Test a design against physical, biological or operational models. | Scenario reports, parameter sets and validation evidence. |
| Collaboration | Share designs, join teams, review work and contribute to projects. | Reviewed contributions, shared workspaces and project lineage. |
| Production / Marketplace | Route reviewed concepts to suitable fabrication or professional services. | Quote requests, production packages and fulfillment records. |

Exploration remains a complete mode. Other modes add specialized tools and workflows while sharing the same player identity, project graph and knowledge base.

## Concept-to-production lifecycle
discover → define → design → simulate → review → prototype → fabricate → inspect → iterate → publish

Each transition has an evidence contract. A design graduates into a professional package when it includes a versioned source model, requirements, dimensions, materials, tolerances, safety review, manufacturing method, quality checks and rights disposition.

## External fabrication and professional services
The provider directory covers 3D printing (FDM, SLA/MSLA, SLS and metal), CNC machining, laser cutting, sheet-metal and waterjet services, PCB fabrication and assembly, robotics/mechatronics integration, materials testing, metrology, product inspection, scientific visualization and licensed laboratory services.

Each provider record includes legal entity, service region, process capability, material constraints, certification/accreditation evidence where applicable, quote interface, lead-time range, file requirements, IP/confidentiality terms, safety constraints, insurance/liability terms and review date.

The platform prepares quote-ready design packages and lets the user review provider options, pricing, terms and delivery details. Purchases, paid professional services and external submissions require explicit user approval. A simulation result remains distinct from a certified engineering design.

## Biological design and laboratory pathway
Biology-related concepts follow a controlled workflow: virtual exploration and simulation, scientific review, biosafety and ethics review where applicable, licensed provider matching, professional review, and user-authorized engagement. Records include organism/context, evidence, uncertainty, intended application, jurisdiction and provider qualifications. Wet-lab services remain a specialist pathway with human professional oversight and applicable institutional controls.

## Internal equipment and shared laboratories
Our equipment fleet is a cross-project capability, modeled as an internal laboratory/automation network (including the AutoMataLabs concept). Equipment records include stable asset ID, owner/custodian, capabilities, work envelope, materials and operating limits, facility location, availability, reservation and project allocation, calibration, maintenance, inspection, operator qualifications, safety controls, consumables, energy, utilization, costs, grant restrictions and reset procedures.

Equipment is shared across owned projects and grant-funded work through a common capability registry. Each reservation is project-scoped and retains grant restrictions, cost allocation, asset custody and audit lineage. Physical operation requires qualified operators and facility approval.

## Project and grant graph
Every concept and production run links to a stable project ID. The graph connects player, team, design, research record, simulation, equipment, grant, provider, quote, purchase authorization, build artifact and inspection record. Public projects publish approved artifacts; confidential work remains in its authorized workspace.

## Production evidence and handoff
A production package carries source design revision, geometry checksum, dimensions/units, materials, process plan, tolerances, machine profile, safety review, QA plan, provider terms and user approval receipt. Final inspection records actual dimensions, material/batch identity, defects, deviations and acceptance disposition.

## First engineering vertical slice
1. Build a compact 3D factory cell with input storage, conveyor/transport, one processing machine, power, sensor and output inspection.
2. Give the player a clear throughput target and constrained energy/material budget.
3. Let the player reconfigure machine order, buffer capacity, transport path and control rules.
4. Display throughput, work-in-progress, bottlenecks, energy, yield and downtime.
5. Export the factory graph and bill of materials as a versioned design artifact.
6. Add a reviewed pathway to a harmless physical prototype, such as a simple 3D-printed fixture or enclosure.

The biological membrane slice remains a parallel science vertical slice. Both slices use the same player profile, project graph, engineering design contracts, visual pipeline and QA architecture.


Cambrian Genomics is retained as a historical DNA laser-printing concept reference. Current company data lists it as out of business, so the project maps current commercial services through separately verified provider candidates rather than treating Cambrian as an available supplier.
