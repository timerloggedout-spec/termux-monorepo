# ELI5 — crosswalk-group-category.mmd

**What the diagram shows:** a set of boxes (ideas from our notation
registry) and arrows between them. Each arrow says: "these two ideas
are connected — here is how."

**What it means, in plain words:**

- A group's "combine two things" operation works like the more
  general idea of chaining steps one after another. Combine = a kind
  of chaining.
- A group's "do-nothing element" (the one that changes nothing)
  plays the same role as the "do-nothing step" in the general
  chaining world.
- Groups themselves form a family where the connections between
  groups are structure-respecting maps — the same kind of arrow the
  general theory uses everywhere.
- The "kernel" of a map is the part it squashes to nothing. The
  diagram shows that this squashed part always forms a special,
  well-behaved subgroup (a "normal" one).

**What the diagram does NOT show:** how sure we are of each
connection, or the evidence for it. Those live in the registry the
diagram was drawn from. The drawing is a picture of the record,
never a replacement for it.
