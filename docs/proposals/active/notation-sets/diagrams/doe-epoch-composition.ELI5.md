# ELI5 — doe-epoch-composition.mmd

**What the diagram shows:** two different ladders that are easy
to mix up, drawn separately on purpose — plus where they touch.

**What it means, in plain words:**

- The top ladder is about *work on a pile of material*. The
  biggest unit is an "epoch": one complete pass over everything
  in the pile. Inside an epoch are cycles (one plan → test →
  check → decide loop), inside cycles are single numbered work
  batches ("iterations"), and inside those, individual runs.
- The bottom loop is about *things competing and improving*. A
  "generation" is one round of: line the contestants up, score
  them, remove the bottom (their value goes into a shared pool,
  it is never destroyed), and start the next round with the
  survivors mixed with fresh entries.
- A generation round happens *inside* one work batch — that is
  the dotted link. And "scoring the contestants" is itself a
  small pass over their records, which is where the two ladders
  genuinely touch.

**The mistake this picture exists to prevent:** finishing a
pass over the pile does NOT mean a generation happened, and a
generation can happen without finishing a pass. Each ladder
keeps its own score.

**What the diagram does NOT show:** the entry and exit rules for
each unit, or this period's actual numbers — including the pass
that never ran, which is why no generation was allowed to turn
over on it. Those live in the definitions document.
