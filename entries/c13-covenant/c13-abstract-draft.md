Teachers are being handed AI outputs they cannot inspect, override, or trust. This proposal builds the missing infrastructure layer: an openly licensed, education-tuned agent harness for formative assessment that any developer can build on, in which every AI-generated question, score, and feedback draft reaches the teacher with a receipt — its sources, model, prompt, confidence, and the student evidence it rests on — and nothing reaches a student without teacher approval.

The harness performs five core tasks: generating assessment items from teacher-supplied learning objectives; interpreting student responses into misconception hypotheses with cited evidence; drafting feedback and next steps under a human-in-the-loop gate; adapting difficulty and sequence to learner variability; and exporting a complete provenance ledger for district review. A receipts discipline proven in our own fleet operations — every claim carries its evidence or is marked unverified — becomes, here, a teacher-facing control surface.

StudySprint, our formative-study application, serves as the demonstration application: proof the harness works in a teacher's hands, deliberately not the center of the work. The majority of effort serves third-party developers — a documented toolkit, reference integrations, and, in Phase 3, at least three independent developers building their own assessment products on the harness.

Across three phases over 18 months we will ship the harness core and receipts layer, run teacher pilots, onboard external developers, and publish everything as a digital public good: code under MIT/Apache-2.0, data and content under CC-BY-4.0. The result is infrastructure that makes AI-enabled formative assessment transparent, controllable, and reusable — for one classroom tool, or for the ecosystem that builds the next fifty.
