# Urdu translation evaluation rubric

Use blinded native or professionally fluent Urdu reviewers. Calibrate reviewers on the same anchor set before scoring the campaign cohort.

## Primary quality score (1–5)

| Score | Anchor | Decision guidance |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Meaning failure | Meaning is materially wrong, misleading, or unusable. |
| 2 | Major repair | Meaning is partly recoverable; substantial rewriting is required. |
| 3 | Adequate with edits | Core meaning is preserved; visible grammar, tone, or terminology edits remain. |
| 4 | Strong | Natural and accurate Urdu; only minor stylistic edits remain. |
| 5 | Publication-ready | Natural, context-appropriate, consistent Urdu with no material correction required. |

## Secondary dimensions
- Adequacy: semantic fidelity to the source.
- Fluency: grammar, idiom, register, and naturalness for the ur-PK profile.
- Terminology consistency: glossary adherence and stable product terminology.
- RTL integrity: correct reading order, punctuation, bidi isolation, and mixed-script handling.
- Accessibility defects: language metadata, focus order, accessible names, and screen-reader interpretation.
- Correction effort: reviewer minutes and edit distance, retained separately from quality.

## Reviewer protocol
1. Randomize task order and blind provider/model identity.
2. Show the canonical English source, Urdu draft, context, and approved glossary.
3. Score primary quality and each secondary dimension independently.
4. Record a concise rationale and corrected text for scores 1–3.
5. Preserve abstentions, disagreements, and unavailable tasks.
6. Report reviewer count, agreement statistic, score distribution, and uncertainty with every summary.

## Decision boundary
A provider ranking informs workflow selection. A designated Urdu reviewer approves each published translation revision. The DOE result itself never approves catalog content.
