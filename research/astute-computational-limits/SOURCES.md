## Primary foundations

2. **Seth Lloyd (2000), “Ultimate physical limits to computation,” Nature 406, 1047–1054.**  
   https://doi.org/10.1038/35023282  
   Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9908043  
   Type: primary theoretical synthesis.  
   Role: computation-rate, information-capacity and physical-resource bounds; ultimate-laptop and black-hole-limit discussion.  
   Caveat: Lloyd explicitly distinguishes theoretical limits from practical attainability.

3. **Norman Margolus and Lev B. Levitin (1998), “The maximum speed of dynamical evolution,” Physica D 120, 188–195.**  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9710043  
   Type: primary theoretical result.  
   Role: lower bound on orthogonal-state evolution time in terms of average energy above the ground state.

4. **Stephen P. Jordan (2017), “Fast quantum computation at arbitrarily low energy,” Physical Review A 95, 032305.**  
   https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.95.032305  
   Type: primary theoretical analysis.  
   Role: important qualification to energy-only interpretations of quantum speed limits as universal computational clock-speed bounds; identifies additional assumptions concerning information density and transmission speed.

5. **Rolf Landauer (1961), “Irreversibility and Heat Generation in the Computing Process,” IBM Journal of Research and Development 5, 183–191.**  
   https://doi.org/10.1147/rd.53.0183  
   Type: primary theoretical result.  
   Role: thermodynamic cost of logically irreversible information processing.

6. **Jacob D. Bekenstein (1973), “Black Holes and Entropy,” Physical Review D 7, 2333.**  
   https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.7.2333  
   Type: primary theoretical result.  
   Role: black-hole entropy and the area relationship.

7. **Jacob D. Bekenstein (1981), “Universal upper bound on the entropy-to-energy ratio for bounded systems,” Physical Review D 23, 287.**  
   https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.23.287  
   Type: primary theoretical result.  
   Role: entropy bound for bounded systems under stated assumptions.

8. **Stephen W. Hawking (1975), “Particle Creation by Black Holes,” Communications in Mathematical Physics 43, 199–220.**  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02345020  
   Type: primary theoretical result.  
   Role: semiclassical derivation of black-hole particle creation / Hawking radiation.

9. **Gerard ’t Hooft (1993), “Dimensional Reduction in Quantum Gravity.”**  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9310026  
   Type: primary theoretical proposal.  
   Role: early holographic-principle argument.

10. **Leonard Susskind (1995), “The World as a Hologram,” Journal of Mathematical Physics 36, 6377–6396.**  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9409089  
    Type: primary theoretical proposal.  
    Role: influential formulation of the holographic principle.

11. **Juan Maldacena (1997/1998), “The Large N Limit of Superconformal Field Theories and Supergravity.”**  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9711200  
    Type: primary theoretical result.  
    Role: AdS/CFT correspondence in its specified setting.

12. **Seth Lloyd (2002), “Computational capacity of the universe,” Physical Review Letters 88, 237901.**  
    https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.88.237901  
    Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0110141  
    Type: primary theoretical estimate.  
    Role: information and operation bounds applied to the universe.

## Current reviews and refinements

13. **Robert B. Mann (2026), “Black-hole thermodynamics,” Nature Reviews Physics 8, 425–436.**  
    https://www.nature.com/articles/s42254-026-00942-9  
    Published 11 May 2026. Type: current review.  
    Role: established thermodynamic principles and open questions including information paradox, black-hole microstructure and holographic approaches.

14. **Krzysztof Andrzejewski, Katarzyna Bolonek-Lasoń and Piotr Kosiński (2024), “Note on the Margolus–Levitin quantum speed limit for arbitrary fidelity,” Quantum Information Processing 23, 167.**  
    https://doi.org/10.1007/s11128-024-04375-0  
    Type: primary theoretical refinement.  
    Role: arbitrary-fidelity generalization and comparison of quantum speed-limit formulations.

15. **Reinhard Genzel, Frank Eisenhauer and Stefan Gillessen (2024), “Experimental studies of black holes: status and future prospects,” The Astronomy and Astrophysics Review 32, article 3.**  
    https://doi.org/10.1007/s00159-024-00154-z  
    Type: observational review.  
    Role: observational techniques and evidence for astrophysical black holes; useful for distinguishing observed black holes from hypothetical computing devices.

In particular, a quantum speed limit, a black-hole entropy formula, a theoretical information-recovery proposal and a realizable computer interface are separate claims. The research map keeps these claims separate and requires independent derivation before numerical values enter the canonical graph.
