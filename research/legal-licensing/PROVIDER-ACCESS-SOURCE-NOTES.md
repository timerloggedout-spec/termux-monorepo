## Free-tier claims versus availability

A list’s “free” label is a lead. It may mean no-cost API calls subject to rate caps, a trial-only/noncommercial key, account-bound credits, an expiring promotion, a shared gateway pool, or a zero-price catalog entry that still requires registration and can be unavailable. “Permanent” is a publisher characterization, not a service-level commitment. Limits may vary by model, region, organization, account age/verification, billing status, and provider policy; a model listing or `/v1/models` response establishes catalog visibility; account invocation and remaining balance require account-level evidence.

Official provider materials illustrate why verification must be per-model and per-account: Google’s [Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing) shows free access only for certain models and explicitly marks free-tier content as usable to improve products; the same page distinguishes paid-tier data handling. Groq’s [rate-limit docs](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits) provide model-specific free-plan limits and say exact limits are organization-visible and subject to exceptions; 429 responses and headers are operational observations; service continuity requires separate measurement. The curated list labels Cohere Trial as noncommercial; Cohere’s [rate-limit docs](https://docs.cohere.com/docs/rate-limits) distinguish free evaluation keys from production keys. Capture current provider source statements and account observations separately. Quota numbers in a directory are mutable observations, not measured remaining account quota. Route legal interpretation requests to qualified counsel.

## Security, privacy, and terms risks

- **Prompt and output exposure:** prompts, retrieved documents, code, and outputs leave our environment for each upstream provider. Free tiers may have different training/improvement, retention, review, and deletion rules than paid tiers. Use public or synthetic inputs for provider experiments; keep secrets, credentials, personal data, and confidential code outside research payloads. Production privacy controls remain engineering-owned.
- **Credential boundary:** keys must be user-authorized, least-privilege where supported, stored only in approved secret storage, redacted from logs, and excluded from catalog artifacts. A proxy’s claim of encryption is not an independent audit; assess local database, backup, admin/UI, network exposure, logs, and key rotation. Use explicit account authorization and approved credential storage. Keep credential, cookie, and session handling under the repository's security controls.
- **Provider terms and identity source statements:** capture published quota, identity, region, account-sharing, and trial conditions as attributed source facts. Record source wording, publication/retrieval date, and uncertainty. Route interpretation and legal consequences to qualified counsel; qualified counsel owns legal verdicts.
- **Reliability and supply chain:** providers can remove models, change quotas/terms, silently alter behavior, or fail. Third-party gateways and auto-updating feeds add intermediaries and provenance risk. Pin and review code/dependencies, verify signatures and source, and run technical security checks before adopting third-party code. Route license interpretation to qualified counsel. Deployment authority remains with the operator and production release process.

## Evidence capture contract

1. Record provider/model identity, exact source URL, source type, retrieval time, source revision, region/account prerequisites, capability evidence, quota cadence, and confidence. Store licensing/terms statements in this silo as source-attributed observations. Preserve `unknown`, `catalog_zero_price`, `documented_trial`, and provider-confirmed account entitlement as distinct technical states.
2. Record capability and availability evidence as observations. Production routing uses independently testable technical capability, account, credential, budget, and privacy controls; legal research informs human review; routing decisions use technical evidence and operator authorization.
3. **Quota-aware failover with cooldown.** Maintain per-provider/model/account request and token windows only where authoritative headers/account APIs support them; preserve `Retry-After` and reset headers. On 429, honor retry delay and cool down that key/model; on transient network/5xx, bounded backoff and retry only where safe/idempotent. Keep each account within its published rate limits and operator-approved usage. Stop on auth, policy, billing, or malformed-request errors rather than retrying blindly. Set per-task attempt, latency, and spend caps; expose the actual provider/model and fallback reason to the caller.
4. **Health and capability gating.** Separate catalog discovery from health checks and inference. Model-list availability and inference health are separate measurements. Use minimal, non-sensitive probes only when authorized, record timestamped outcomes, and open/close a circuit breaker with bounded recovery probes. Remove unavailable models from eligible routing without rewriting source evidence.
5. **Audit and privacy controls.** Log metadata (provider/model, request ID, timing, quota evidence, decision reason); keep prompts, secrets, cookies, and raw sensitive payloads outside telemetry. Keep credentials in secret stores and use explicit retention limits. Treat provider response text as untrusted input.
